Why does the sky go orange, then pink, then purple? It comes down to one simple idea: **the longer light travels through air, the redder it gets.**

## Air steals the blue

Sunlight is a mix of every colour. Air scatters blue light sideways far more than red. At midday the sunlight crosses only a little air, so the blue scattered all around makes a blue sky and the sun looks white.

Near the horizon the light has to cross many times more air. Most of the blue is scattered away on the journey, and what reaches you is yellow, then orange, then red.

## Clouds are screens

A cloud has no colour of its own. It shows whatever light falls on it, like a cinema screen. At sunset that light has already crossed a lot of air, so the cloud glows orange or red.

## Why pink comes late

After the sun has set for you, it still lights the clouds higher up. But to reach them, its beam has to skim low over the ground on the way, crossing hundreds of kilometres of thick air.

The closer Earth's shadow gets to a cloud, the lower that beam has to dive, and the redder and dimmer it arrives:

- beam stays high: **gold or bright orange**;
- beam dips lower: **orange, then orange-red**;
- beam skims the ground: **deep red and pink**, and fading fast;
- beam would touch the ground: the cloud is in shadow and goes **grey**.

skyglow tracks exactly how low that beam dips. That is the colour you see in the strips and the readout.

## The higher the cloud, the longer the show

High clouds sit further above Earth's shadow, so they stay lit longer. For a cloud straight overhead:

| Cloud height | Goes dark when the sun is this far below the horizon |
|---|---|
| 2 km | about 1.4° |
| 6 km | about 2.5° |
| 10 km | about 3.2° |

Near the equator the sun drops about a quarter of a degree a minute, so a 10 km cloud stays lit roughly 7 minutes longer than a 2 km one.

## What makes a great sunset

- **High clouds overhead and a clear gap toward the sun**, so the light can reach them.
- **Low cloud on the sun's side** spoils it: it blocks the light before it reaches anything.
- **A little haze** deepens the colour; **a lot** turns everything grey.

## Honest note

The geometry is exact. The colour names are rough guesses for now, and they will get better as people log real sunsets.
