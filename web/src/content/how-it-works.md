skyglow answers two questions about any place on Earth: **when** does the sky do its best colours, and **which clouds** will catch them.

## The sun is completely predictable

Where the sun sits in the sky, at any minute, anywhere, is plain arithmetic: the Earth spins once a day, circles the sun once a year, and leans 23.4 degrees. skyglow works it out to within a few seconds, with no internet.

That gives you sunrise and sunset, and how fast the sun moves near the horizon. Near the equator it drops almost straight down, so the whole show lasts about 20 minutes. Far north it slides sideways, and twilight can last an hour or more.

## The evening has stages

After sunset the sky does not switch off. It moves through stages, set by how far below the horizon the sun is:

- **Golden hour**: low, warm light while the sun is still up.
- **Sunset glow**: orange and red on low and middle clouds.
- **Afterglow**: only high clouds are still lit, often pink and magenta. Frequently the best part.
- **Purple light**: a fading violet glow.
- **Blue hour**: deep blue, colour mostly gone.

In the morning the same stages run backwards, and the highest clouds light up first.

## Clouds stay lit after the sun has gone

When the sun sets for you, it is still shining on the air high above you. Earth's shadow then climbs up the sky, and each cloud stays lit until the shadow reaches it. Low clouds go grey first, high clouds last. That is why the best pink often arrives several minutes *after* sunset.

On the **colour strips**, each row is one cloud height: you can watch it go from daylight to gold, to pink, to dark.

## Where you stand matters too

Standing higher lets you see a little further over the curve of the Earth, so your sunset comes slightly later. Mountains in the sun's direction hide it early, while clouds above keep glowing. Tell skyglow your height and skyline, and it allows for both.

## What it cannot know yet

The timing is exact. What decides whether the evening is spectacular or grey is the air: whether clouds are actually there tonight, how hazy it is, how humid.

When you are online, the **Conditions** card shows a weather forecast for exactly that: cloud cover at three heights, humidity and haze. The **Glow forecast** card turns it into a rough score from 0 to 10 for each cloud height, finds the best moment around sunrise and sunset, and shows every reason behind the number. It is built from public rules of thumb for now; learning from real photos is the next big step.
