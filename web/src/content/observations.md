Skyglow's timing is exact, but its **colour guesses** need real evenings to learn from. Every sunset you note down helps.

## What to note

A few lines are enough. For example:

- **18:05**: low clouds already grey, high wispy clouds bright orange.
- **18:09**: high clouds turning pink, edges glowing.
- **18:12**: pink fading, sky going purple.

Times from your photos are the most accurate. Add roughly how high the clouds looked (low, middle, high) if you can.

## Compare with the model

Set Skyglow to the same place and date, and drag the **time scrubber** to each time you noted. The readout tells you what the geometry says the sky should be doing at that minute. Where it matches, the guesses are good. Where it does not, that is exactly the data that improves them.

## Keep your spots private

Where you watch sunsets is often close to home. If you share an observation, consider using a nearby town, or coordinates rounded to one decimal place (about 11 km), instead of your exact spot. The sky does not need the exact spot to learn from it.

## Morning and evening are different

Dawn air is usually cooler, damper and cleaner than evening air, so morning and evening colours may follow slightly different rules. Note which one you saw.
