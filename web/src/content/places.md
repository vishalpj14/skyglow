The sky is the same for everyone nearby, but **what you see depends on where you stand**. Skyglow uses three things about your spot.

## Where you are

Your latitude and longitude decide where the sun is and how fast it sets. Pick a place by typing its name, using your phone's location, or tapping a map.

## How high you are

From higher up you can see a little further around the curve of the Earth. The horizon sits slightly below eye level, so the sun takes longer to reach it and your sunset comes a bit later (and your sunrise a bit earlier).

From a 2,000 m hill the difference is a few minutes near the equator. Skyglow fills in the ground height for you when it can; you can always type your own.

## What is in the way

A ridge or mountain toward the sun hides it before it reaches the true horizon. From a valley you can lose the sun an hour or more early, while clouds overhead still glow. That glow over a shaded valley is one of the best sights there is.

You can describe your skyline under **Skyline** on the place card, in two ways:

- **Horizon points**: "toward the west the skyline is 12° high". Measure with a phone app or by eye.
- **Peaks**: a mountain's direction, distance and height, read off a map. Skyglow works out how high it looks from where you stand.

## Saving places

Tap **Save on this device** to keep a place. It stays in this browser only and is never sent anywhere.

## Honest note

A skyline here is a handful of numbers, not a 3D model of the land. Treat mountain results as a good guide, and check your own spot against a real sunset.
